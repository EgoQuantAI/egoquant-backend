How we calculated this

  1. Market history. We took the real daily price changes of the 11 investments in this portfolio from 2007-05-31 to 2026-10-16 (about 19 years, 4,877 trading days), a period that includes the 2008-09 financial crisis, the 2020 COVID crash and the 2022 fall of stocks and bonds together.
  2. 10,000 possible futures. Each simulated future is built month by month: we pick a random stretch of 21 consecutive trading days (about one month) from that history, then another, and so on for 10 years. Whole months keep crashes and rebounds together, the way they really happened.
  3. Market regimes. A machine-learning model (a Hidden Markov Model fitted on the daily returns and volatility of the S&P 500 fund SPY and the US bond fund AGG, 2007 to 2026) sorts every past trading day into one of four market regimes: calm, normal, stressed or crisis. Each simulated future moves from regime to regime month by month with the chances the model learned, and each month is drawn only from past months that began in the same regime, so a crisis lasts as long as crises really did instead of being broken up. Market regime today: normal (probability 98%). In about 77 of every 100 simulated futures, there is at least one crisis period lasting 3 months or more.
  4. Your plan in each future. Each month the portfolio earns that month's returns (rebalanced back to its mix), pays its fund fees, and then you add nothing more. Money cannot go below zero.
  5. Counting the futures. We count the futures that end at or above 80,000 USD: 7,802 of 10,000, which is 78%.
  6. Corrections against over-optimism. Stocks, stock funds and crypto-assets (5 in this simulation) are picked from today's most traded list, so their past returns flatter them (survivorship bias). We kept their real ups and downs but set their average return to what their sensitivity to the world stock market earned (beta times the market's average). Investments younger than the history used (4 in this simulation) had their missing early years rebuilt from the long-history funds they move with, adding no extra return.
  7. The same futures for every option. Every shortlisted portfolio was tested on exactly the same 10,000 futures (fixed random seed 20261017), so differences come from the portfolios, not from luck, and the same inputs always give the same result.
