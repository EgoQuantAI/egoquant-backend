## Agent C: market intelligence (which products, which prices, and how honest we are about both)

**In plain words.** Before any portfolio can be built, someone has to decide *which products this investor is allowed to hold* and *what their past prices were*. That is Agent C's whole job. It does not choose weights (Agent A does), does not judge suitability (the risk profile and the Risk Engine do), and never says buy or sell. Its output is a list of products with a written reason for every inclusion and exclusion, plus a table of past prices, plus a plain statement of where those prices came from. If the live price vendor fails, Agent C falls back to a saved copy of **real** prices, says so in the report, and never mixes two sources in one build. Code: `egoquant/agents/market_intel/` (`agent.py`, `catalog.py`, `directory.py`, `platforms.py`, `sources.py`, `snapshot.py`, `yahoo.py`, `warm.py`); tests: `tests/agent_c/` (254 tests) and the files named below.

### 1. The product list and its risk levels
- **One catalog, 292 products** (`catalog.py`): 4 cash funds, 23 bond funds, 57 stock funds, 4 property funds, 180 large US single stocks, 14 commodity products, 4 crypto funds and 6 crypto pairs. Four "core" funds (BIL cash, SHY short government bonds, AGG broad bonds, VT world stocks) are always considered.
- **The product risk level (R1 to R5) lives only here.** Rule: cash and money-market R1; government and investment-grade bonds R1 to R3; stock funds R3 or R4; property funds and every single stock R4; crypto R5. `tests/agent_c/test_market_intel.py::test_every_catalog_rating_follows_the_r_level_rule` checks every one of the 292. (Counts: R1 5, R2 18, R3 25, R4 234, R5 10.)
- **A larger directory** (`directory.py`): about 4,450 US-listed stocks and funds, sorted by name, sector and market value into the same interest areas by fixed rules (no AI); leveraged, inverse, options-strategy, volatility and note products are excluded. The live system rebuilds it weekly in the background and never inside a request; until it is loaded, and offline, the committed catalog is used. `tests/agent_c/test_directory.py` (65).

### 2. From the investor's answers to a list of products
Inputs, all validated fields (never raw chat text): the interest areas (29 to choose from, up to 8 used per build: `PORTFOLIO_MAX_INTEREST_AREAS`), the trading platforms, the maximum product risk level and the blocked product types from the risk profile.
1. **Platform rule** (`platforms.py`): a crypto exchange (Binance) trades crypto pairs; a US broker (Interactive Brokers, Charles Schwab, Fidelity, Robinhood, Futu/moomoo, Tiger, or "other US broker") trades US-listed products. The platform decides what can be bought and where its price comes from. Hong Kong or China-A shares are not built.
2. **Eligibility:** a product above the investor's maximum risk level, or of a type their knowledge blocks (crypto, for investors who did not show the knowledge), or an asset class Agent A cannot model, is **not offered**. It goes on a **watchlist**: priced for the news page and drawdown alerts, never sent to Agent A. Every decision (included, excluded, watchlist) carries a sentence saying why.
3. **Walk the interest areas, most traded first.** A product that cannot be priced is replaced by the next one in the *same* area. Recent performance is **not** used to rank anything (that would be a forecast). Caps (PROVISIONAL, `agent.py`): 25 per area, 50 single stocks, 30 crypto assets, 100 products in all.
4. **"No stocks chosen" rule:** an investor who chose no stock area gets a stock cap of 0 instead of a forced world-stock fund (`tests/system/test_no_stocks_chosen.py`).

### 3. Price sources and the fallback chain
- **Live:** Tiingo for US-listed products (end-of-day adjusted prices; the free plan allows about 50 requests an hour and 500 distinct tickers a month, so a build may spend at most **40 new requests**, cached prices are free), Binance's public market-data API for crypto pairs. Two Tiingo keys keep the background cache builder from taking the requests investors need (`warm.py`).
- **Time budgets:** a build spends at most 15 s waiting for live prices (`EGOQUANT_LIVE_BUDGET_SECONDS`); after 6 s no new request is made for an optional product (`PORTFOLIO_PRICE_FETCH_SECONDS`); one request slower than 8 s counts as slow, two slow ones switch the whole build; two "cannot reach the vendor" in a row switch it at once (`UNREACHABLE_IN_A_ROW`, measured: 191 s before, about 10 s after). A rate-limit answer (429) is not a failure: Agent C stops asking that feed for 15 minutes and keeps what it has (`TIINGO_COOLDOWN_SECONDS`).
- **The fallback, in this order:** live feeds, then the **committed snapshot of real prices**, then synthetic prices *only* if someone switches that on (`EGOQUANT_DATA_FALLBACK=synthetic`, off by default). **The whole build uses one source.** `EGOQUANT_PRICE_MODE=snapshot` skips the live feeds entirely (no request of any kind); this is the mode recommended for the live system while the price vendor's quota and the host's disk cache cannot be relied on.
- Tests: `tests/agent_c/test_data_fallback.py`, `test_slow_feed_and_snapshot_mode.py` (12), `test_fetch_time_limit.py`, `tests/test_slow_feed_drill.py` (18), `tests/test_ibkr_price_fallback.py`, `tests/agent_c/test_cache_write_failure.py` (a cache folder that cannot be written no longer breaks a build).

### 4. Where the saved prices come from (provenance)
| File (`egoquant/agents/market_intel/data/`) | Content | Read by |
|---|---|---|
| `prices_snapshot_yahoo.json.gz` | Adjusted closes of all 292 catalog products, 2007 to 16 Oct 2026; the six crypto pairs from Binance's public data. Downloaded **once, offline, on 19 Oct 2026** with the `yfinance` library (`yahoo.py`, `python -m egoquant snapshot --source yahoo --include-stocks`) | Agent C's fallback |
| `prices_snapshot.json.gz` | 112 funds and crypto pairs from Tiingo, 23 May 2007 to 16 Oct 2026, complete (no missing ticker) | the regime model (the prices it was fitted and evaluated on; a test checks who reads which) |

- The running system **never calls Yahoo**: `yfinance` is not in `requirements.txt` and no module imports it (`tests/agent_c/test_yahoo_source.py::test_the_app_never_depends_on_yfinance`).
- Cross-check of the two files on the same funds: median total-return gap 0.06% over 19 years; daily-return correlation of SPY 0.99999; one bad print in each file (CPER in Yahoo's, ITA in Tiingo's). Agent C has no spike filter (listed as a limitation).
- **Licence honesty:** Yahoo has no licence for this data and `yfinance` states personal and research use; the team accepted it for this competition. A commercial product would need a licensed feed (`docs/SECURITY.md` T17).
- A snapshot is used only up to 30 days after its date (`max_snapshot_age_days`); older, Agent C says so and tries the live feeds. If the Yahoo file is unreadable it tries the Tiingo file; a damaged file is a clean "market data unavailable" (HTTP 503), never a crash (`tests/agent_c/test_price_snapshot.py::test_a_damaged_snapshot_file_is_a_source_error_never_a_crash`).

### 5. Cleaning the prices (before Agent A sees them)
`agent.py::_clean`: nothing dated after the decision day is ever used (no look-ahead); duplicate days are dropped; non-positive or non-numeric prices become missing; weekend rows are removed; gaps of at most 3 days are filled and counted (`filled_values` in the report); a price series that stops too early is dropped as stale; a product with less than about a year of history (253 rows) is dropped; a product listed much later than the core funds is trimmed instead of cutting everyone's history; all series are aligned to a common start for the covariance. Everything dropped is listed with its reason (`SnapshotReport.dropped`). Agent A still validates what it receives.

### 6. Data-honesty labels (what the investor and the judge can read)
- `data_source`: **"historical"** (real prices) or **"synthetic"**; synthetic data carries a banner and exists only in tests and demos.
- `price_note` and the report banner "Prices: saved snapshot of real prices as of 16 Oct 2026, Yahoo Finance..." appear when the saved copy was used, **naming only the feeds of the products actually priced for this investor** (a funds-only portfolio does not credit Binance: `tests/agent_c/test_price_note_feeds.py`, `tests/system/test_price_note_in_reports.py`). `prices_as_of` gives the date the prices end, `fallback_reason` a safe one-line cause (never an error text). The same date and provider reach the advisor's facts, the trade-check notes and the saved-report summary (`tests/system/test_snapshot_banner.py`, `tests/accounts/test_saved_summary_prices.py`).
- Every historical number is "in-sample"; every stress result "hypothetical"; drawdown alerts are phrased as "what changed" and can only *offer* a risk-profile re-check.

### 7. Limits of Agent C
Single stocks reach only investors whose class allows risk level R4 or higher; the stock list is today's most traded, so survivorship bias is corrected later in the goal simulation (see the goal section) rather than here; the free Tiingo plan limits how many products one build can price from live feeds (the saved snapshot covers all 292); the host has no persistent disk, so the live price cache is wiped on each deployment (reason for `EGOQUANT_PRICE_MODE=snapshot`); a Binance block from a US server would remove only the crypto pairs; the real download of the product directory could not be tested offline. A 60-year goal build holds about 150 MB (Agent A's simulation, not Agent C), which is why portfolio builds run one at a time (`04_TESTING_AND_EVIDENCE.md` section 7).
